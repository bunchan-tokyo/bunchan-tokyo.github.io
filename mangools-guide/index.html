The problem: publishing blind

When you write a post without first checking what people are actually searching for, you risk publishing content that no one sees. A solo blogger can spend hours drafting and editing, yet the article may still miss its target audience because it addresses a keyword that is rarely searched or that is dominated by higher-ranked sites.

Beyond search volume, the difficulty of ranking for a keyword matters too. If the competition level is too high, a new post is unlikely to climb the SERPs, even if the topic is relevant to your niche. Without that information, you're essentially guessing whether your effort will pay off.

That guessing problem is the whole reason SEO tools exist. The question for a solo blogger or creator is not "which tool is most powerful?" — it's "which tool answers my three or four recurring questions without charging me for an enterprise platform?" Those recurring questions are usually:

  1. What should I write about next? (keyword research)
  2. Can a site like mine realistically rank for it? (SERP analysis)
  3. Are my existing posts moving up or down? (rank tracking)
  4. Who links to my competitors, and can I get similar links? (backlink analysis)

Mangools is built as five separate, focused tools that map almost one-to-one onto those questions. Below is what each one does, what it costs, and — just as important — where it stops being the right choice.

Start with the keyword: KWFinder

KWFinder is part of the Mangools suite and lets you explore more than 2.5 billion keywords, view keyword difficulty scores, and filter by location across 65k+ places worldwide. A solo blogger can start a post idea by entering a seed keyword, then quickly look up related terms, see how hard it would be to rank, and pick a more attainable keyword to focus on.

One honest limitation is the daily lookup cap: the free tier only allows five keyword searches each 24-hour period, while the Basic plan raises that to 100. If you're on the free plan, you'll need to be selective about which keywords you investigate.

In a solo workflow, this is a "before you write" tool: ten minutes in KWFinder before drafting can save you from spending five hours on a post that targets a keyword with either no searches or only authority sites ranking for it. It also surfaces the keywords competitors already rank for, which is often the fastest way to find topics your niche is proven to search for.

Check the battlefield: SERPChecker

Finding a promising keyword is only half the decision. SERPChecker shows you the actual search results page for that keyword — in a specific city, on mobile or desktop — with 45+ metrics for each ranking page: authority scores, backlink counts, estimated CTR, and which SERP features (featured snippets, videos, "People also ask") are taking up space.

For a solo blogger, the practical use is a go/no-go check: if the top ten is all major publications with huge link profiles, you move on. If you see forums, small blogs, or outdated posts, that's a realistic opening. Mangools keeps 30M+ SERPs in its database and offers downloadable snapshots of real SERPs, which helps when you want to study a result page without triggering a fresh lookup every time.

Track what you already published: SERPWatcher

Most solo creators check rankings by googling their own keywords in a normal browser — which shows personalized results and tells them almost nothing reliable. SERPWatcher tracks your chosen keywords properly, imports data from Google Search Console, and produces shareable interactive reports (with a Looker Studio connector and whitelabel reports for client work).

The honest caveat is update frequency: rankings update weekly on the Basic and Premium plans. Daily updates require the Agency plan. For a blogger publishing weekly, weekly tracking is usually fine. If you run client campaigns or react to algorithm updates day by day, it will feel slow — and that alone can justify a different tool.

Borrow your competitors' link profile: LinkMiner and SiteProfiler

LinkMiner explores competitors' backlinks from an index of 9.5 trillion links, with filtering and a live preview that shows exactly where a link is placed on the referring page — useful for judging whether a link is worth replicating or is just a footer mention. SiteProfiler is the quick-overview companion: enter any domain and get its authority/popularity metrics, backlink profile summary, and its most popular content.

For a solo blogger, the realistic use is modest but valuable: find two or three sites ranking just above you, see which pages link to them, and build a short, honest outreach list. Neither tool will do outreach for you, and neither replaces creating something worth linking to.

Who Mangools is for — and who should skip it

A good fit if you:

  • Run a blog or content site on your own or with one or two collaborators
  • Are new to SEO tools and want an interface you can understand on day one (this is Mangools' most consistent praise in third-party reviews, and its UI is genuinely its selling point)
  • Mainly need keyword research, SERP checks, rank tracking, and light backlink analysis in one place
  • Want the lowest-priced entry point among the established suites

Look elsewhere if you:

  • Need daily rank updates without paying for the top tier (Basic/Premium update weekly)
  • Need a deep technical site audit — the suite is five focused tools; a dedicated technical crawling/auditing tool is not one of them
  • Do high-volume research (agency-scale lookups will hit the daily caps even on Agency)
  • Need a broad digital-marketing platform (ads, social, content marketing) rather than an SEO toolkit

How it compares: SE Ranking and Semrush

Semrush is the heavyweight: a broad all-in-one marketing platform that goes well beyond SEO. Its Pro plan starts at around $140/month (US, check the official site for current pricing) — more than double Mangools Basic's monthly price. For an agency or a marketing team, that breadth can be worth it. For a solo blogger who will use maybe 10% of it, it is usually paying for shelfware.

SE Ranking sits between the two: an all-in-one SEO platform with a strong reputation for rank tracking and plans that scale with how many keywords you track. It is a reasonable step up if you outgrow Mangools' weekly updates or lookup caps. (We have not listed SE Ranking prices here because its pricing depends on the tracking volume and check frequency you select — use its official pricing page to price your exact setup.)

Bottom line

If you are a solo blogger or creator who keeps publishing without data, Mangools Basic — or even the free account first — fixes the most expensive habit (writing posts nobody searches for) at the lowest entry price of the three. Buy it for KWFinder and SERPChecker; keep it if SERPWatcher's weekly tracking matches your publishing rhythm. Skip it, and look at SE Ranking or Semrush instead, if you need daily tracking, technical audits, or marketing features beyond SEO.
